Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026

MultiTasker has no account and no server of its own. What it knows about your tasks and what you're working on stays on your computer, or goes to the services you choose to connect, such as Todoist, Notion or your own cloud storage. Nothing is sold, shared for advertising, or used to track you.

This policy covers the MultiTasker app for Windows, the MultiTasker browser extension for Chrome, Edge and Brave, and this website. MultiTasker is open source, so you can check everything described here in its source code.

The app

Your tasks

Tasks, checklists, notes on where you left off, time spent and the files and links you attach are kept in a database on your computer. If you choose to use Todoist or a Notion database instead, MultiTasker reads and writes your tasks there, through those services' own APIs, with the token you give it. Their own privacy policies then apply to what's stored there.

Noticing what you're working on

So it can tell which task you're on, MultiTasker looks at which program and window is in front, the files you save in folders you've linked to a task, and whether you've touched the keyboard or mouse recently. This happens on your computer only. None of it is recorded or sent anywhere; only the outcome (which task is in progress, and for how long) is kept with your tasks. You can turn this off in Settings.

Syncing between computers

Syncing is off until you turn it on. When you do, MultiTasker writes your tasks and settings to the place you choose: a folder carried by a sync app, OneDrive, Google Drive, or S3-compatible storage. With OneDrive and Google Drive it can only reach its own app folder, not your other files. MultiTasker talks to these services directly from your computer; nothing passes through anyone else.

Handed-in work

When you hand in a file, MultiTasker keeps a copy on your computer, and in your task list or sync location if you use one, so you have every version. When it checks that a link you're handing in can be opened by others, it opens that link the way a visitor without your sign-in would.

Passwords and tokens

Sign-ins and tokens for the services you connect are kept in Windows Credential Manager, not in MultiTasker's own files, and are only ever sent to the service they belong to.

Updates

The app checks GitHub for a newer version when it starts and every six hours. Like any visit to a website, this lets GitHub see your IP address. No information about you or your tasks is included.

Diagnostics

MultiTasker doesn't collect analytics or crash reports. A small log of the browser connection is kept on your computer to help work out problems; it stays there unless you choose to share it.

The browser extension

The extension only ever talks to the MultiTasker app on the same computer, through the browser's native messaging. It doesn't send anything to any server.

  • It tells the app the address and title of the tab in front, so the app can tell which task you're on and offer to link the page. When you link a page, its address and title are kept with that task.
  • When you start a task, it can switch to that task's pages, or open them, at the app's request.
  • Reply notices are off until you turn them on in the extension's popup, which is when the browser asks for the extra permissions they need. Then, on the AI chat sites listed below, the extension watches whether a reply is being written and notices when requests to those sites start and finish. It never reads, changes or blocks those requests or their contents, and nothing from those pages leaves your computer: the app only learns that a reply started and finished, and on which tab. Turning reply notices off gives the permissions back.
  • It keeps a small record on your computer of when it last noticed a reply on each of those sites, so it can tell you if one of them has changed in a way it can't follow.

The AI chat sites reply notices work on are claude.ai, chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, gemini.google.com, aistudio.google.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com, chat.deepseek.com and grok.com.

Permissions it asks for

  • nativeMessaging: to talk to the MultiTasker app on your computer.
  • tabs: to read the address and title of the tab in front, and to switch to a task's tabs.
  • storage: for the record of noticed replies, kept on your computer.
  • scripting, webRequest and access to the AI chat sites above: only once you turn on reply notices, and only to tell when a reply is finished.

This website

This website is a set of static pages. It uses no cookies, no analytics and no tracking, and its fonts are served from this site rather than a font service. The company hosting it may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses and the pages requested, for running and protecting the site. Downloads come from GitHub, under GitHub's own privacy policy.

Children

MultiTasker isn't directed at children, and it doesn't knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If what MultiTasker does with your information changes, this page will be updated before the change is released, and the date at the top will change. Past versions are in the project's history on GitHub.

Questions

Ask anything about this policy, or about MultiTasker and your information, by opening an issue on GitHub.
